2004

George Siemens introduces connectivism online, proposing that learning involves forming connections among people, information sources, and digital tools.

2005

Siemens publishes “Connectivism: A Learning Theory for the Digital Age,” emphasizing diverse perspectives, maintaining learning networks, and recognizing when information changes (Siemens, 2005).

2008

George Siemens and Stephen Downes offer “Connectivism and Connective Knowledge” (CCK08), an early massive open online course that demonstrates learning through distributed resources, participant contributions, and network connections (Downes, 2009).

2008

Rita Kop and Adrian Hill publish a critical examination of connectivism, questioning whether it constitutes a distinct learning theory or an emerging approach to teaching and learning (Kop & Hill, 2008).

2011

Rita Kop investigates learning in an open online network, identifying challenges involving learner autonomy, educator presence, and the critical literacies needed for participation (Kop, 2011).

2012

Stephen Downes publishes “Connectivism and Connective Knowledge: Essays on Meaning and Learning Networks,” expanding his explanation of distributed knowledge and learning through connections (Downes, 2012).
